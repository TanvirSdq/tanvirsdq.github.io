1. Who I Am: A Fresher with a History of Building

I am currently a first-year undergraduate in Computer Science & Engineering at Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), just about three months into my degree. But my journey with programming didn't start at university.

During my college years at Notre Dame College, Dhaka, I got involved in open-source automation as part of the Notre Dame English Club Wikipedia Editorial & Research Team (NDEC WERT). Instead of just reading articles, I started writing Python scripts using the official Wikimedia framework (Pywikibot) to automate real community operations:

  • Structuring the Fulani Wikipedia: The newly created Fulani (Fula) Wikipedia had roughly 10,000 articles without proper structure. I wrote scripts to automatically assign around 25 core categories (people, places, events) based on English Wikipedia mapping patterns.
  • Welcoming New Editors: Automated welcome messages and onboarding on English Wikipedia talk pages.
  • Event Data & Analytics: Processed and visualized participation and image data for Wikimedia Commons photo competitions (Wiki Loves Folklore, Wiki Loves Earth), culminating in WebApp, an interactive Streamlit dashboard to evaluate campaign health and editor retention.

Across these tools, I ran over 46,000 automated edits on live Wikipedia projects. It taught me how to handle real-world API limits, messy data, and write code that runs reliably without breaking live platforms.

I don't use C for large production software yet, and I don't pretend to. As a university fresher, C is where I build my daily problem-solving discipline.

Through my repository guide, I practice solving competitive programming problems from Codeforces (like Watermelon, Way Too Long Words, Next Round, Beautiful Matrix, and Domino piling) alongside my university lab work. Writing C every day keeps me close to the basics: handling standard I/O, writing clean loops and conditions, working with strings without high-level shortcuts, and building a clear mental model of how data actually lives in memory. It is my daily foundation.

3. Shipping Under Pressure: CampusShare KUET

When it comes to building complete products, I like solving immediate friction.

During the 50-hour ForkedArch Freshers Hackathon 2026, my team noticed a constant problem in KUET's residential halls: students frequently need academic items like scientific calculators, drafting tools, multimeters, lab coats, and books, but have no quick or safe way to borrow them from peers.

Over the 50 hours, we designed and built CampusShare KUET:

  • Backend & Database: A fast, asynchronous API using FastAPI and PostgreSQL to handle users, listings, requests, and transactions.
  • Frontend: A clean, responsive interface in React 18 so students can browse and post items from their phones or laptops.
  • Campus Map: An interactive radar map using MapLibre GL showing equipment availability across KUET halls.
  • Verified Handover: A dynamic QR code and 6-digit OTP verification system so an item can't be marked as borrowed or returned without both students confirming in person.
  • Karma Protocol: A simple built-in trust score (+10 for lending, +5 for returning on time, −30 for late returns) to keep borrowers accountable.

We deployed it live on Render. It showed me how much a focused team can ship in a weekend when everyone is aligned on solving a real problem.

4. Machine Learning: Understanding the Math, Not Just the Wrappers

I am actively studying machine learning and data science. Rather than just copying someone else's notebook, I want to understand what is happening under the hood:

  • From Scratch (MLP-Implementation): I built a character-level Bigram Language Model in PyTorch on The Wizard of Oz text from scratch—manually creating the character lookup tables, computing cross-entropy loss, running backprop with AdamW, and sampling text with temperature. Building it tensor-by-tensor gave me a clear picture of how language models actually learn token predictions.
  • Computer Vision (MainCropDiseaseDetection): Trained an image classifier using transfer learning with EfficientNet to recognize 38 crop disease classes from leaf photos, wrapped in a Streamlit app with confidence scores.
  • Competitive Practice (Kaggle & U-Tokyo GCI 2026): Currently participating in the University of Tokyo Matsuo-Iwasawa Lab GCI World 2026 course and working through classic tabular competitions (Titanic, Spaceship Titanic, House Prices, Fraud Detection) to master clean feature engineering, cross-validation, and gradient boosting (XGBoost, LightGBM).

5. Where I Am Heading: Systems Programming Meets ML

Here is the direction I am committed to: I want to master ML/AI, but from a systems-level perspective.

A lot of developers learn AI by importing Hugging Face models or calling API endpoints. My goal is different. I want to know how the pieces are built:

  • Writing Tokenizers from Scratch: Building my own BPE (Byte-Pair Encoding) and WordPiece tokenizers in C/C++ or pure Python, handling unicode, regex splitting, and vocabulary mergers without relying on external libraries. This is a project I am starting soon.
  • Low-Level Implementations: Writing basic matrix math, custom loss functions, and neural network layers from scratch to understand computational cost and memory usage.
  • Systems Rigor: As I progress through my CSE coursework at KUET, combining what I learn about operating systems, memory, and data structures with machine learning pipelines to build fast, lightweight, and practical software.
