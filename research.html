Building from First Principles: Systems Rigor, Real-World Scale, and Machine Intelligence

First-year Computer Science & Engineering undergraduate at KUET bridging low-level systems programming in C, full-stack product execution, and foundational machine learning.

"Software engineering is most powerful when grounded in physical reality—where algorithms respect hardware constraints, automated pipelines maintain absolute data integrity at scale, and end-to-end applications eliminate tangible friction for real humans."

1. The Builder's Instinct: High Agency and Precocious Velocity

Long before attending my first formal computer science lecture, I was hooked on building software that solves immediate, tangible bottlenecks. During my college years at Notre Dame College Dhaka, I didn't wait for permission or curriculum milestones to build. As part of the NDEC World Environment and Resource Team (NDEC WERT), I started designing automation scripts and data workflows to handle large-scale environmental media and event documentation.

Now, three months into my undergraduate journey in Computer Science & Engineering at Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), I approach university not as the starting point of my engineering life, but as an intensive proving ground to deepen my mathematical and low-level theoretical foundations. My trajectory is defined by a simple principle: build software from first principles, master the machine from the hardware up, and deploy solutions that work reliably in the hands of users.

2. Foundations First: Systems Rigor, Memory Control, and C Algorithms

High-level abstractions are convenient, but true engineering confidence comes from understanding the bare metal. Over the past several months, I have anchored my programming foundation in POSIX C99, methodically solving algorithmic problems from first principles through my repository guide and competitive platforms like the UVa Online Judge.

Writing C forces an engineer into direct contact with the machine:

  • Zero Runtime Dependencies: Implementing dynamic arrays, graph representations, tree traversals, and dynamic programming tables without external utility libraries or garbage-collected runtimes.
  • Manual Memory Discipline: Managing pointers, dynamic allocations (malloc, free), memory leaks, and struct alignment by hand, developing an instinct for memory layouts and heap versus stack lifecycles.
  • Cache Locality & Execution Efficiency: Developing an intuitive mental model for CPU cache lines, spatial and temporal locality, and computational complexity.

While I build production web applications using modern stacks like Python, FastAPI, and React, I dedicate time every single day to C and algorithmic problem solving. Understanding how memory is laid out and how hardware actually executes instructions ensures that I never treat software as magic.

3. Real-World Scale & Automation: Wikimedia Infrastructure Pipelines

Algorithmic puzzles in controlled environments are only half the battle. Production engineering requires wrestling with dirty data, flaky networks, API rate limits, and idempotent state transitions.

Through my automation engine TanvirSdqBot and open-source contributions, I engineered large-scale automation pipelines that operate directly on global knowledge infrastructure:

  • Structuring Fulani Wikipedia: Automated the taxonomic restructuring of the entire Fulani (Fula) Wikipedia (ff.wikipedia.org), programmatically generating, categorizing, and interlinking approximately 10,000 articles across 25+ core subject categories.
  • 46,900+ Production Bot Edits: Executed nearly 47,000 automated edits using Pywikibot and MediaWiki APIs across Wikimedia platforms with zero vandalism rollbacks or state corruption.
  • Media Asset Ingestion & Cleanup: Standardized and categorized over 15,800 global media files and event datasets on Wikimedia Commons (including international photo competitions and college heritage archives).
  • Campaign Analytics Suite (WebApp): Built an analytical dashboard with Streamlit, Pandas, and Plotly to ingest real-time MediaWiki event logs, compute a 5-dimension weighted campaign health score, and benchmark regional metrics across global Wikimedia campaigns.

This work taught me the realities of data engineering: building idempotent scripts that can resume gracefully after network timeouts, respecting distributed API rate limits, and taking complete responsibility for modifications made at scale.

4. End-to-End Product Execution: Shipping CampusShare KUET in 50 Hours

When real user friction arises, high agency means delivering a complete, production-grade product under pressure. During the 50-hour ForkedArch Freshers Hackathon 2026, my team recognized an acute problem on our university campus: students in KUET's residential halls frequently need expensive or scarce academic equipment—scientific calculators, engineering drafters, multimeter kits, lab coats, and reference textbooks—yet have no reliable, transparent way to borrow them from peers.

In 50 hours, we engineered and shipped CampusShare KUET, a complete full-stack peer-to-peer sharing ecosystem:

  • Full-Stack Architecture: Built an asynchronous, high-throughput backend using FastAPI backed by a normalized PostgreSQL database via SQLAlchemy, paired with an interactive, responsive React 18 client.
  • Real-Time Geospatial Radar: Integrated MapLibre GL to render an interactive campus radar map displaying equipment availability across individual student halls and academic departments in real time.
  • Cryptographic Handover Verification: Designed a dispute-free physical exchange protocol utilizing dynamic QR codes and 6-digit cryptographic OTPs, ensuring items cannot be marked as borrowed or returned without mutual, verified confirmation by both parties.
  • Game-Theoretic KUET Karma Protocol: Implemented an automated reputation and trust scoring engine featuring time-decay mechanics, deposit staking, and penalty tiers to disincentivize late returns and prevent equipment loss.

Shipping CampusShare KUET under intense hackathon time constraints proved my ability to execute full-cycle delivery: identifying user friction, designing system schemas, implementing secure transactional logic, and polishing frontend user experience without cutting corners on reliability.

5. Machine Learning: From Foundations to Applied Diagnostics

My approach to machine learning mirrors my approach to systems programming: I do not settle for being a passive consumer of pre-packaged black boxes. I study machine learning from mathematical foundations, implement core algorithms from scratch, and deploy applied models to solve practical problems.

  • Foundations from Scratch (MLP-Implementation): Built a character-level Bigram Language Model in pure PyTorch from first principles on The Wizard of Oz corpus. Rather than calling high-level training abstractions, I implemented token embeddings, negative log-likelihood loss computation, backpropagation mechanics, softmax probability normalization, and temperature-controlled generation from scratch. Building models from the tensor level demystifies the underlying linear algebra and gradient flow.
  • Agricultural Computer Vision (MainCropDiseaseDetection): Implemented a deep learning image classification pipeline utilizing transfer learning with EfficientNet to identify 38 distinct plant disease classes across major agricultural crops. Deployed the trained model through an accessible Streamlit web application with confidence score thresholding to provide real-time diagnostic assistance for farmers and agricultural extension workers.
  • Competitive Tabular Discipline (Kaggle & UTokyo GCI World 2026): Actively participating in the University of Tokyo Matsuo-Iwasawa Lab Global Consumption Intelligence (GCI) World 2026 program. Honing rigorous data science practices: advanced feature engineering, out-of-fold cross-validation strategies to prevent data leakage, and hyperparameter optimization across gradient boosting frameworks (XGBoost, LightGBM, Scikit-Learn).

6. Where I Am Heading: The Convergence of Systems and Machine Intelligence

The engineering space I am most passionate about mastering is the intersection of Systems-Level Programming and Machine Learning/AI.

The rapid rise of modern AI has created an ocean of engineers who can write high-level Python wrappers, but a severe shortage of engineers who understand how neural workloads actually execute on physical silicon. My roadmap over the coming years is centered on building intelligence infrastructure from the bottom up:

  • Raw Tokenizers from Scratch: Moving past off-the-shelf tokenizer packages to build byte-level Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) and subword tokenization engines in pure C/C++, focusing on deterministic regex splitting, prefix trees, and zero-allocation string buffers.
  • Custom Tensor Operations & Compute Kernels: Writing hand-crafted tensor arithmetic, matrix multiplication routines, and activation kernels, studying cache tiling, SIMD vectorization, and memory bandwidth utilization.
  • Memory-Efficient Model Execution: Investigating quantization algorithms (INT8/INT4), memory-mapped weights, and execution runtimes designed to serve machine learning models efficiently on resource-constrained edge hardware.

My long-term ambition is to build fast, robust, production-grade intelligence infrastructure where strict systems fundamentals—predictable memory, minimal latency, and zero bloat—empower the next generation of computing.
